\begin{abstract}
Contactless heart-rate sensing from a webcam is usually evaluated on its own, yet it is increasingly paired with language models trusted to reason about clinical risk straight from raw vitals. \textbf{VITAL} tests both assumptions together. Its rPPG pipeline -- \textbf{CHROM} corrected by a small gradient-boosted model -- reaches \textbf{6.48 BPM mean absolute error} across \textbf{71 real sessions} from UBFC-rPPG and UBFC-Phys, down from \textbf{9.43 BPM} classically -- an aggregate gain concentrated in a subset of hard sessions, not a uniform per-subject effect, landing mostly where three DSP fixes could not reach: motion artifact from talking. Separately, we asked whether VITAL's language-model narration layer can be trusted to simply repeat a number it was handed. Across \textbf{16 runs} on \textbf{8 controlled clinical scenarios}, it echoed the correct priority score verbatim only \textbf{6.2\% of the time}, even when explicitly told not to recompute it. That result is why a \textbf{deterministic scoring engine}, not the language model, remains the \textbf{sole source of truth} for acuity, shock status, and priority in VITAL. We report what worked, what failed, and where this system still trails the closest published rPPG benchmark.
\end{abstract}

\section{\textbf{Introduction}}

\subsection{\textbf{Background and Motivation}}
Emergency departments triage patients under time pressure with a static, sensor-dependent workflow, and every minute spent attaching a sensor to a waiting patient is a minute deterioration can go unnoticed. \textbf{Remote photoplethysmography (rPPG)} sidesteps this: heart rate, HRV, and respiratory rate can be pulled from an ordinary webcam feed with no contact at all \cite{b_chrom, b_pos_principles, b_pirzada_review}. Meanwhile, \textbf{large language models} are increasingly proposed as the reasoning layer that turns those vitals into a triage decision, often through multi-agent frameworks splitting perception, diagnosis, and coordination across separate LLM agents \cite{b_warrier_agentic, b_sharma_triagebot}. It is an attractive pairing -- a webcam and an LLM API in place of a nurse's stopwatch and a static rule table -- but it raises a question rarely measured directly: when an LLM agent is handed a patient's exact clinical numbers and told only to restate them, \textbf{does it actually do so?}

\subsection{\textbf{Problem Statement}}
Two gaps motivate this work. The first is methodological: rPPG systems are often validated once, against a clean benchmark signal, without the harder work of tracing why specific real subjects fail and fixing the cause rather than tuning a global threshold. The second is more consequential -- agentic-AI clinical tools increasingly let an LLM see raw vitals and reason toward a severity classification directly \cite{b_liu_tsai_cds}, without checking whether it reliably preserves a number it was only meant to \textbf{restate, not recompute}. Warrier and Abhilash's agentic orchestration framework for hospital workflows \cite{b_warrier_agentic} builds in a runtime assurance layer to veto unsafe agent actions -- a guardrail their architecture addresses procedurally, \textbf{not empirically}.

\subsection{\textbf{Contributions}}
This paper makes five contributions, each backed by a measured result:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item A complete, deployed contactless-vitals-to-triage pipeline -- webcam capture, \textbf{MediaPipe} ROI extraction, a \textbf{CHROM} rPPG engine, a \textbf{deterministic clinical scoring engine}, a three-agent \textbf{CrewAI} narration layer, and a Next.js/Flask/Neon Postgres stack -- running end to end, not a benchmarking script.
    \item \textbf{Three independent classical-DSP bugs} in the rPPG engine, each root-caused by tracing a specific failing subject rather than tuning a global parameter: a harmonic lock-on bug, a hardcoded-frame-rate bug, and a history-aggregation freeze bug. Combined, they took the real-video baseline from \textbf{17.4 to 8.4 BPM MAE} on the original 56-session set.
    \item A \textbf{hybrid classical-DSP + gradient-boosted correction layer}, validated with subject-level \textbf{GroupKFold}, reducing real-video MAE from \textbf{9.43 to 6.48 BPM (31\% relative reduction)} across \textbf{71 sessions and 51 subjects} -- concentrated in a subset of hard cases (Section~\ref{sec:results}), largest in speech, the one condition DSP-level fixes never touched.
    \item A deterministic scoring engine expressing \textbf{Compensated Shock} as a \textbf{multiplicative interaction} between tachycardia and tachypnea ramps rather than a boolean AND-gate, with an explicitly enforced architectural rule that this engine alone is the \textbf{sole source of truth} for the numbers driving triage priority.
    \item A direct, repeated measurement of whether an LLM agent faithfully restates a given clinical number: across \textbf{16 real CrewAI runs}, the priority score was restated correctly only \textbf{6.2\% of the time}, even under an explicit ``do not recompute'' instruction -- turning the guardrail need identified in \cite{b_warrier_agentic} into a concrete number.
\end{enumerate}

\section{\textbf{Related Work}}

\subsection{\textbf{rPPG Signal Extraction}}
\textbf{CHROM} \cite{b_chrom} and \textbf{POS} \cite{b_pos_principles} remain the two dominant classical rPPG projection methods. Pirzada et al.'s \textbf{2024 survey of over 300 rPPG studies} found both still sensitive to motion artifact and lighting change, with the field mostly validated in labs, not realistic settings \cite{b_pirzada_review} -- the same sensitivity traced here in the speech-condition failures. A \textbf{2026 clinical validation} of a commercial system in 47 cardiovascular patients reports \textbf{1.06 BPM MAE} against ECG \cite{b_chin_clinical}; that gap is a large part of why this paper does not overstate its own.

\subsection{\textbf{ML-Corrected and Hybrid rPPG}}
The closest published comparator to VITAL's hybrid layer is \textbf{Qaadan et al.}, combining five rPPG methods with four classical regressors across four datasets under a single 80/20 split \cite{b_qaadan}. Their best result -- CHROM into XGBoost with permutation-importance feature reduction -- reaches \textbf{4.60 BPM MAE / 5.57 RMSE}, ahead of this project's comparable numbers (Section~\ref{sec:results}). Their conclusion names \textbf{``hybrid algorithmic-learned models''} as future work they never built \cite{b_qaadan}; that is exactly VITAL's architecture.

\subsection{\textbf{Agentic and Learning-Based Emergency Triage}}
A 2025 review of interpretable ML for ED triage found \textbf{gradient-boosted models} dominate (AUROC 0.78--0.96), flagging gaps in operational-metric evaluation and fairness \cite{b_jonatha_iml}. Yaddaden et al.'s \textbf{Polynomial Features transform} reaches \textbf{99.80\% accuracy} for ESI prediction on MIMIC-IV \cite{b_yaddaden} -- the same idea applied here, multiplying a tachycardia ramp by a tachypnea ramp. \textbf{Triage-Bot} pairs an rPPG extractor with a rule-based CTAS classifier \cite{b_sharma_triagebot}, evaluation still pending. Liu and Tsai pair explainability with a \textbf{RAG pipeline} \cite{b_liu_tsai_cds}; VITAL's retrieval is simpler, term-frequency cosine similarity. None checks whether an LLM layer's output \textbf{reproduces a clinical number faithfully}.

\subsection{\textbf{Research Gap}}
No system we reviewed combines root-caused DSP fixes, a subject-level cross-validated correction layer, an enforced deterministic/LLM boundary, and a repeated faithfulness measurement. The correction layer closes a gap Qaadan et al. name as unbuilt future work \cite{b_qaadan}; the faithfulness measurement turns a guardrail need the agentic-orchestration literature addresses only procedurally \cite{b_warrier_agentic} into an actual number.

\subsection{\textbf{Overview}}
VITAL runs as \textbf{three layers} on a live Flask API and Next.js dashboard, backed by Neon Postgres. A \textbf{perception layer} extracts a face ROI via MediaPipe Face Mesh (Haar-cascade fallback) and feeds normalized RGB means into an rPPG DSP engine. A \textbf{clinical-reasoning layer} deterministically computes a custom ESI-style acuity score (Section~\ref{sec:clinical-engine}), a Compensated Shock flag, and a priority score. A \textbf{narration layer} -- three CrewAI agents -- restates that output for the dashboard and ARIA, a voice assistant with read-only database access. Fig.~\ref{fig:architecture} summarizes the pipeline.

\subsection{\textbf{Perception Layer}}
MediaPipe's \cite{b_mediapipe} \textbf{468-point Face Mesh} isolates forehead and malar-cheek landmarks with yaw-aware gating, so an off-angle cheek region is excluded rather than blended in noisily. The layer also estimates \textbf{ambient luminance} and \textbf{centroid drift} per frame, both exposed to the correction model as candidate features.

\subsection{\textbf{rPPG DSP Engine}}
The default projection is \textbf{CHROM} \cite{b_chrom}: per-channel normalization, projection onto two chrominance axes, and adaptive scaling by the projected-signal standard-deviation ratio. A \textbf{Welch periodogram} locates the dominant heart-rate frequency; \textbf{cubic-spline upsampling to 250 Hz} supports RMSSD-based HRV; a 0.10-0.50 Hz bandpass extracts respiratory rate. \textbf{POS} \cite{b_pos_principles} is a hot-swappable alternative, \textbf{not wired into any live route} (Section~\ref{sec:pos-results}).

\subsection{\textbf{Deterministic Clinical Engine}}
\label{sec:clinical-engine}
The \textbf{deterministic clinical scoring module} computes \textbf{ESI-style acuity}, the \textbf{Compensated Shock flag}, and a \textbf{1-100 priority score} directly from extracted vitals in plain code -- never sampled from an LLM. Rather than a boolean gate that simply checks whether heart rate exceeds 100 bpm and respiratory rate exceeds 20 breaths/min, treating a barely-elevated reading the same as a severe crisis, severity above that gate is graded by a \textbf{multiplicative interaction}:
\begin{align}
\text{tachy}_{HR} &= \text{ramp}(HR,\ \text{threshold}=100,\ \text{span}=40) \\
\text{tachy}_{RR} &= \text{ramp}(RR,\ \text{threshold}=20,\ \text{span}=15) \\
\text{shock}_{\text{interaction}} &= \text{tachy}_{HR} \times \text{tachy}_{RR}
\end{align}
where $\text{ramp}(v, t, s)$ rises linearly from 0 to 1 as $v$ climbs from $t$ to $t+s$. Multiplying rather than averaging or OR-ing means risk accumulates only when both vitals are elevated together, matching the physiological definition of \emph{compensated} shock as a combined cardiorespiratory response. HRV (RMSSD) and a \textbf{Baevsky Stress Index} are folded in as secondary, lower-weight terms, and any Compensated Shock flag unconditionally forces manual review. We describe this deliberately as a \textbf{custom, vitals-only scoring rule}, not a faithful ESI \cite{b_esi} or NEWS2 \cite{b_news2} implementation -- both need inputs (chief complaint, SpO$_2$, blood pressure, temperature) a webcam cannot measure.

\subsection{\textbf{Multi-Agent Narration Layer}}
Three CrewAI agents run in sequence: a \textbf{Perception Agent} restates already-extracted vitals, a \textbf{Diagnostic Agent} explains the ESI level and shock flag using retrieved guideline context, and a \textbf{Coordinator Agent} restates the priority score. Guideline grounding uses \textbf{term-frequency cosine similarity}, simpler than comparable RAG pipelines \cite{b_liu_tsai_cds}. Each request spins up a fresh crew, since CrewAI's task objects store output internally and are not safe to re-enter concurrently.

\textbf{Why the LLM does not compute the ESI level.} An earlier revision let the agents freely generate the ESI level and shock flag as JSON, falling back to a hardcoded default of 3 on any parse failure -- making the most safety-critical number non-deterministic. Section~\ref{sec:faithfulness} presents the measurement that motivated reverting this.

\section{\textbf{Dataset Description}}

Real-video validation draws on \textbf{71 sessions across 51 unique subjects}, pooled from two public datasets \cite{b_ubfc_rppg, b_ubfc_phys}. The three DSP bugs (Section~\ref{sec:methodology}) were root-caused on an initial \textbf{56-session} set; \textbf{15} more UBFC-Phys sessions, found later already on disk, were folded in through the same fixed pipeline. They were harder on average -- the classical baseline rose from 8.42 to \textbf{9.43 BPM MAE} once included -- so every Section~\ref{sec:results} number reflects the harder pool, not the easier subset the bugs were found on.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{UBFC-rPPG} \cite{b_ubfc_rppg} -- \textbf{41 sessions} (both public releases combined), a math-stress protocol with ground truth from a contact pulse-oximeter waveform.
    \item \textbf{UBFC-Phys} \cite{b_ubfc_phys} -- \textbf{30 sessions} (10 subjects $\times$ 3 conditions: rest, speech, arithmetic, a Trier Social Stress Test-inspired protocol), ground truth from a wrist-worn BVP waveform, peak-detected into a per-session BPM.
\end{itemize}
Class composition is uneven by design (10 rest, 10 speech, 10 arithmetic, 41 unlabeled UBFC-rPPG), reported stratified rather than pooled into a misleading average (Table~\ref{tab:per-condition}). Every model uses \textbf{GroupKFold by subject identifier} -- no subject's sessions appear in both folds -- keeping the correction model from \textbf{memorizing a specific person}.

\section{\textbf{Methodology}}
\label{sec:methodology}

\subsection{\textbf{Classical DSP Bug-Fixing, By Tracing Individual Failures}}
Rather than tuning global thresholds against an aggregate metric, each bug below was found by tracing one specific failing subject to its root cause, then verified against the full real-video set. Table~\ref{tab:bugs} summarizes the three bugs identified.

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\caption{Classical DSP Bugs, Root-Caused and Fixed}
\label{tab:bugs}
\begin{tabular}{@{}p{1.7cm}p{2.7cm}p{3.5cm}@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Bug} & \textbf{Root Cause} & \textbf{Fix, Verified Effect (BPM)} \\
\midrule
Harmonic lock-on & Peak-picker locks onto half the true rate for sustained stretches & Promote to the double when it has comparable power; subj.41 52.6$\to$8.2, subj.46 41.2$\to$1.3, subj.9 60.9$\to$2.8 \\
Hardcoded frame rate & Every engine assumed 30 fps regardless of the real source rate & Read the true source fps; subj.25 23.5$\to$3.5, subj.26 25.5$\to$2.5 \\
History freeze & An 18 BPM noise-rejection gate can anchor on an early wrong burst forever & Allow re-anchoring only while the anchor is still small; subj.37 21.0$\to$6.0, no regression elsewhere \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

The history-freeze fix needed a second look: a first attempt fixed its target subject but \textbf{silently regressed the harmonic-lock-on fix's flagship case} to a 41.8 BPM error, revealing the earlier ``fixed'' result had been propped up by this same freeze bug anchoring correctly by luck, not design. A narrower fix resolved both without re-breaking either. Together, all three fixes moved the 56-session baseline from \textbf{17.4 to 8.4 BPM MAE (52\% relative reduction)}; RMSE 24.1$\to$15.6.

\subsection{\textbf{Hybrid Classical-DSP + ML Correction}}
A \textbf{gradient-boosted regressor} (\texttt{GradientBoostingRegressor}, \textbf{Huber loss}) corrects CHROM's per-cycle BPM output using \textbf{ten features per cycle}: raw BPM, signal-quality index, SNR, a stability score, respiratory rate and its confidence, HRV, the Baevsky stress index, and the adaptive filter's cutoff frequencies -- each a causal, real-time output of the DSP engine, computed only up to that cycle, so none carries information from later in the session. The model is evaluated with \textbf{5-fold GroupKFold by subject}, so every session-level result in Section~\ref{sec:results} comes from a model that never saw that subject during training. Huber loss was adopted after comparing it against squared-error loss on the same folds, producing a small but consistent gain (\textbf{8.80$\to$8.67 BPM} per-cycle MAE, \textbf{6.65$\to$6.48 BPM} session-level MAE) -- consistent with squared-error over-weighting a handful of extreme-outlier sessions. Larger models -- a bigger regularized neural network, a bigger gradient-boosted model, an ensemble of both -- \textbf{did not beat the small deployed model}; the binding constraint is \textbf{51 unique subjects}, not labeled-cycle count. One honest caveat: hyperparameters are fixed constants, never tuned inside the GroupKFold loop, but the \textbf{loss-function choice} was a form of \textbf{model-selection bias} -- chosen by comparing performance on the same 71 sessions later reported as the final result.

\subsection{\textbf{POS's Own Bug, Partially Fixed}}
\label{sec:pos-results}
POS \cite{b_pos_principles} shows a \textbf{mirror-image failure to CHROM's}: it overshoots to roughly double the true rate from cold start. A symmetric ``demote to the half'' check cut POS's synthetic MAE from \textbf{26.71 to 16.00 BPM (28.88 RMSE)} with zero effect on CHROM -- real but partial, two of seven synthetic cases still fail. On real video, tested here for the first time, results were mixed: one subject accurate (1.7 BPM error), two others failed oppositely, and no threshold fixed either -- one subject's true rate never appears as a candidate, the other's is present but weak (median \textbf{11\% power}, never crossing 30\%). The cause is structural: CHROM applies global mean-normalization and variance equalization before projecting; POS applies only per-window mean-normalization \cite{b_qaadan}. Fixing this needs a rewrite of POS's projection math, so it stays \textbf{experimental}.

\subsection{\textbf{Agent Narration Faithfulness Evaluation}}
\label{sec:faithfulness}
To test whether the LLM narration layer can be trusted with clinical numbers directly, we ran the real CrewAI pipeline -- live API calls to an \textbf{8-billion-parameter} instruction-tuned LLM -- against \textbf{8 controlled clinical scenarios} spanning the acuity range (normal, isolated tachycardia, isolated tachypnea, mild shock, severe shock, bradycardia, low signal-quality, no-signal), each run twice for \textbf{16 total runs}, checking whether the LLM's JSON output exactly matched the deterministic assessment on every field despite instructions to restate values verbatim.

\subsection{\textbf{Subject-Level Significance}}
The \textbf{31\% relative MAE reduction} is real, but not uniform. Averaging each subject's sessions first, only \textbf{30 of 51 subjects} show any net improvement; the bootstrap \textbf{95\% CI} on the mean subject-level gain is \textbf{[-0.19, 4.54] BPM}, crossing zero, and a paired Wilcoxon test does not reach significance (\textbf{p=0.24}). The top 5 subjects alone, corrected by 15-36 BPM each, account for \textbf{101.7\%} of the total gain -- the rest roughly cancel out. The correction layer's value is rescuing specific hard failures, not improving every session; we report this rather than let the aggregate imply otherwise.

Even told to restate rather than recompute, the priority-score field was rarely a plausible miss. In \textbf{9 of 16 runs} the LLM substituted the patient's own \textbf{ESI level integer} into that field (e.g. \texttt{2} for a true score of \textbf{58}) -- a repeatable \textbf{field-confusion error}, not drift; the rest mixed stray decimals with non-numeric text (\texttt{"Resuscitation"}, \texttt{"Emergent"}). Checking whether the true value appeared anywhere in the raw output, to rule out a schema artifact, still gave \textbf{1/16 (6.2\%)} -- a substituted wrong value, not a formatting slip. The LLM also called tools that do not exist, a second gap. This is a small, 16-run sample under one 8B configuration: a failure of \textbf{that configuration}, not LLMs generally.

\section{\textbf{Discussion}}

\subsection{\textbf{What the Numbers Actually Mean}}
Table~\ref{tab:faithfulness}'s result shows this safety rule was \textbf{necessary, not merely cautious}. Trusting the LLM's own JSON for the priority score would have produced an incorrect priority-score value in \textbf{more than 9 of every 10 runs}. Comparable agentic-orchestration architectures guard against this failure procedurally, without measuring how often an agent's output diverges from ground truth \cite{b_warrier_agentic}; this is what that divergence looks like, measured.

The hybrid model's largest gain sits where DSP fixes could not reach: speech's motion artifact is \textbf{orthogonal} to harmonic ambiguity and frame-rate mismatch, so a learned correction absorbs a bias no heuristic can express -- also why bigger models did not help, since the real limit is \textbf{51 subjects}, not cycle volume.

\subsection{\textbf{Positioning and Limitations}}
Not an accuracy paper competing on a single metric -- Qaadan et al.'s is a pure rPPG-accuracy benchmark with no deployed system, clinical-safety layer, or agent-reliability measurement, and Table~\ref{tab:qaadan-compare} shows they win on their own metric. Real limits remain: \textbf{no real-data respiratory-rate accuracy} (unvalidated RR feeds both the ML features and shock-interaction term); \textbf{no skin-tone-stratified accuracy}; a \textbf{single-session} live-camera path; and a \textbf{custom, vitals-only} rule, not validated ESI or NEWS2 \cite{b_esi, b_news2}. To be precise: this paper validates \textbf{rPPG accuracy} and \textbf{LLM faithfulness}, not clinical triage accuracy.

\section{\textbf{Conclusion and Future Work}}

VITAL demonstrates a contactless vitals-to-triage pipeline: a hybrid correction layer \textbf{closes 31\% of the real-video accuracy gap} left by classical rPPG (\textbf{9.43$\to$6.48 BPM MAE}, 71 sessions) -- real, concentrated in a subset of hard subjects, not a broad effect -- and a deterministic clinical engine stays the \textbf{sole source of truth} for triage-critical numbers, justified by measuring that the LLM narration layer restates priority score correctly only \textbf{6.2\% of the time}. Three root-caused DSP bugs, one partial POS fix, and the failed fixes are reported alongside a real gap against the closest benchmark, not hidden.

Future work: closing the \textbf{named feature gap} against \cite{b_qaadan} (skewness, autocorrelation, sample entropy, spectral centroid); a \textbf{structural rewrite} of POS's projection step; \textbf{skin-tone-stratified validation} once compatible metadata exists; and a \textbf{live, real-patient pilot}, out of scope here but planned. Also open: a \textbf{nested-GroupKFold} Huber re-evaluation, and \textbf{expanding the LLM faithfulness run} beyond 16 trials.
