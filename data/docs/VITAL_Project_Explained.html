PROJECT EXPLAINED

VITAL

Vision-Based Intelligent Triage & Lifesign Analytics — contactless vitals triage from an ordinary webcam

The project in one paragraph

VITAL measures a patient's heart rate and breathing rate from a webcam, with no contact and no special sensor. A plain-rules clinical engine turns those vitals into an Emergency Severity Index (ESI) level, a Compensated Shock flag and a queue priority. Three AI agents then explain the result in plain language, and the patient joins a priority-sorted dispatch queue. The numbers are decided by rules, never by the AI.

~3.9 BPMheart-rate error (MAE), 125 recorded sessions, 75 people
~1 br/minrespiration error (MAE), three recorded datasets
0 AIlanguage-model involvement in ESI, shock or priority

Accuracy figures are from recorded video and clinical datasets, not yet from a live-webcam study.

1. Problem and objectives

The problem

  • In a busy emergency department, triage depends on a nurse measuring heart rate and breathing by hand or with contact devices. In a crowded waiting room, a seriously ill patient can wait unseen.
  • Contact monitors need staff time and equipment for every patient; a webcam is cheap and already everywhere.

Objectives

  • Measure without contact: extract heart rate and breathing rate from ordinary video.
  • Decide by rules, not by AI: compute ESI acuity and the Compensated Shock flag with a deterministic, tested engine.
  • Explain the decision: have AI agents write a clear clinical narrative that a triage nurse can trust, grounded in ESI guideline text.
  • Prioritise the queue: keep every assessed patient in a priority-sorted dispatch board backed by a cloud database.
  • Validate honestly: measure accuracy on real datasets with people the models never trained on, and report what did not work.

Reading the diagram

  • Top to bottom is the path of one patient: the screen asks the API, the API feeds frames to the processing modules, their numbers go to the AI layer, and the finished record goes to the database.
  • The orange block is the only decision-maker. Everything above it produces measurements; everything below it explains or stores a decision that has already been made.
  • Everything is optional except the rules. If the ensemble models, the AI models or the database are unavailable, the app degrades step by step instead of stopping (see section 4).
  • Data flows both ways in time: frames stream continuously (about 30 per second), while the triage run happens once, when the measurement window ends.

3. How it works, step by step

Thirteen points follow the data from the screen the nurse watches, down through the signal processing, and back up through triage to the dispatch queue.

3.1 Vitals Monitor (frontend)

In simple wordsThe screen the nurse watches: live video, the numbers and the pulse wave.
  • A Next.js page (BedsideMonitor.tsx) polls the backend /status every 300 ms and shows the camera as an MJPEG stream.
  • It shows HR, RR, the Baevsky stress index and signal quality (SQI), with a live pulse (pleth) trace.
  • Alarm limits are printed beside each number and mirror the clinical engine: HR > 100 or < 50, RR > 20, stress > 150 (elevated > 80), SQI < 40. The monitor decides nothing clinical; it only displays what the backend reports.

3.2 Flask API and session state

In simple wordsThe traffic controller that pulls every frame through the processing chain.
  • web/routes.py::generate_frames() loops once per frame: get the frame → feed the signal processing → update the metrics → stream the image.
  • SessionState holds the histories and live metrics behind a processing_lock, so threads cannot corrupt shared data. One workstation = one camera = one live session.
  • Uploads are decoupled from playback: /upload only stores a file; /api/start_uploaded_video starts it. A fresh AdvancedRPPG engine is built per session.
  • Files use the file's own real frame rate. A live camera uses the resampler in point 3.5.
  • Main endpoints: /video_feed, /status, /upload, /start_webcam, /api/triage_run, /api/triage_queue, /api/chat, /api/generate_report.

3.3 Camera and ROI extraction (core/camera.py)

In simple wordsFind the face, then measure how its skin colour flickers with each heartbeat.
  • OpenCV captures frames. A Haar-cascade detector finds the face. The newer MediaPipe face mesh is not available in the installed version, so Haar is what really runs.
  • Three skin patches are used: forehead (weight 0.5) and both cheeks (0.25 each). Each frame gives one averaged (R, G, B) value.
  • A frame-to-frame motion delta is computed and used for quality gating and as a model feature.

3.4 rPPG engine (core/rppg.py, class AdvancedRPPG)

In simple wordsEach heartbeat pushes blood into the skin and changes its colour by a tiny amount. We extract that tiny signal and find its rhythm.
  • Ring buffer of the last 10 s of RGB samples.
  • CHROM projection (de Haan): normalise each channel by its mean, X = 3R − 2G, Y = 1.5R + G − 1.5B, pulse S = X − (σX/σY)·Y. This cancels the skin-tone and lighting part common to all channels.
  • POS projection: S1 = G − B, S2 = −2R + G + B, pulse = S1 + (σS1/σS2)·S2. Used as a second, independent view of the same frames.
  • Band-pass (zero-phase Butterworth) over 42–180 BPM; once a rate is confirmed the band narrows around it (adaptive filter).
  • Welch spectrum with a Hann window; the strongest peak is the heart rate.
  • Harmonic fixes: a peak at half or double the true rate is the classic error; a sub-harmonic promotion check corrects it.
  • BPM lock (hysteresis) only moves when the evidence is consistent; a history gate rejects readings far from the recent median, with an escape hatch for a bad cold start.
  • Session answer: the IQR-filtered median over all accepted readings.

3.5 Live-camera resampler (core/live_resampler.py)

In simple wordsA webcam does not deliver a perfectly steady 30 frames per second. We re-time its frames onto a steady 30 Hz clock so the maths stays correct.
  • The engine converts every frequency to BPM using one fixed frame rate and ignores timestamps. A webcam driver reports a nominal 30 fps, but the real loop delivered about 10 fps on the development machine.
  • That scaled every frequency by about 3× (heart rate came out 51–147 BPM for a true 98–111) and starved respiration of samples.
  • Fix: live frames are resampled by timestamp onto a uniform 30 Hz grid before they reach the engine. Large gaps (face lost, stalled camera) restart the grid instead of inventing data. Uploaded files are unchanged.
  • Verified on recorded sessions rebuilt as camera streams at 6, 8, 10, 15, 20, 24, 30, 45, 60 and 120 fps: heart rate stayed within about 1–2 BPM of the native answer at every rate. Below roughly 6 fps a pulse cannot be recovered.

3.6 HR ensemble (core/hr_ensemble.py)

In simple wordsInstead of trusting one spectral peak, two different models vote on which peak is the real heart rate.
  • A second engine runs POS on the same frames, so every cycle has two 116-bin spectra covering 0.70–3.00 Hz.
  • Peak selector: 200 gradient-boosted trees score up to a few candidate peaks per cycle on 29 features (peak power, rank, harmonics, signal quality, motion…) and pick the best.
  • Spectro-temporal tracker: a convolutional network over the last 11 spectra, one second apart, asks whether a peak persists over time. It was pre-trained on a GPU physiological simulator, then fine-tuned on real recordings.
  • The answer is the mean of the two session medians. Both models are exported as plain numpy arrays, so the app needs no torch or scikit-learn, and they run on a worker thread so the video never stalls.
  • If the model files are missing or fail, the older hybrid (ML-corrected) and classical path takes over automatically.

3.7 Respiration rate (RIV fusion)

In simple wordsBreathing leaves a faint trace on the pulse: it changes the pulse's height and baseline.
  • Each heartbeat is detected and two respiratory-induced variations are extracted: RIAV (pulse amplitude) and RIIV (baseline intensity).
  • Each series is resampled to a uniform 4 Hz with a cubic spline; this also removes the pulse itself, which sits far above 2 Hz.
  • The search band adapts to the heart rate. The two channels are filtered and fused by the median over a 60 s window, updated about twice a second.
  • Confidence comes from how well the two channels agree. A reading below 30% is suppressed, so you see "--" instead of a wrong number. Session RR is the IQR median of accepted readings.

3.8 HRV, stress and signal quality

  • HRV is the RMSSD of beat-to-beat intervals (reported clipped to 5–100 ms). The Baevsky stress index summarises how narrow the interval distribution is.
  • SQI (signal quality index) is a weighted blend of five checks: spectral peak concentration 40%, kurtosis 20%, entropy 20%, zero-crossing regularity 10%, autocorrelation 10%.
  • A low SQI lowers the confidence of the triage result and is shown to the nurse.

3.9 Clinical Rule Engine (core/clinical.py) — the decision-maker

In simple wordsA plain-rules calculator, with no AI, that decides who is most urgent.
  • Limits: tachycardia HR > 100, bradycardia HR < 50, tachypnea RR > 20, HRV < 20 ms is low, stress index > 80 elevated and > 150 critical. Children use age-specific limits.
  • Compensated Shock = HR > 100 and RR > 20 together. Any shock flag forces manual review, as does every patient under 18.
  • Risk score = 0.55·(HR term × RR term) + 0.25·max(HR term, RR term) + 0.12·stress term + 0.08·HRV term.
  • ESI: shock with risk ≥ 70 → ESI 1. Shock, or risk ≥ 55, or marked bradycardia → ESI 2. One elevated vital or high stress → ESI 3. Weak signs → ESI 4. None → ESI 5.
  • Priority (1–100) = ((6 − ESI)/5 × 0.7 + risk/100 × 0.3) × 100, so the queue is a smooth ranking and not five buckets.
  • If heart rate cannot be read, the result defaults to ESI 3 with manual review. If respiration alone is missing, the engine still assesses on heart rate (shock needs both).
Worked example (demo clip): HR 99.6 and RR 11 are both normal, but the stress index was 122. Stress term = (122 − 80)/70 = 0.6, so ESI 3 (stress ≥ 0.5). Risk = 0.12 × 0.6 ≈ 7.3, priority = (0.42 + 0.022) × 100 = 44.

3.10 Triage run API (/api/triage_run)

  • Collects the session vitals (ensemble first, then hybrid, then classical) and runs the rules engine; retrieves matching ESI guideline text for the agents.
  • The ESI level, priority and shock flag stored in the record are always the engine's output, never parsed from AI text. If the AI crew fails, deterministic narrative text is stored and triage still completes.
  • The patient and a queue token are saved to Neon in a single statement; a unique index guarantees two patients can never get the same token.

3.11 Clinical Crew (CrewAI, agents/crew.py)

In simple wordsThree AI writers who explain the decision. They cannot change it.
AgentJob
Perception — Sensor Perception & Signal SpecialistRestates the measured vitals exactly, without inventing values.
Diagnostic — Clinical Diagnostic SpecialistExplains why this ESI level was assigned, in clear clinical language, using the retrieved guideline text.
Coordinator — Emergency Department CoordinatorWrites the one-line queue summary; its ESI, priority and shock fields must match the engine's exactly.
  • The three run sequentially. The model is chosen by agents/llm_pool.py, which probes NVIDIA-hosted models, ranks the live ones and drops dead ones, so a model being retired does not break the app. If the whole pool is down, the deterministic result still stands.
  • Why the AI is kept out of the numbers: in a measured test of 16 real runs, the model restated the priority score correctly only 6.2% of the time (ESI level 81.2%, shock flag 93.8%), even when told to copy it.

3.12 Triage Dispatch and the queue

  • The dispatch board lists every assessed patient from the Neon PostgreSQL database, sorted by ESI first and priority second, with colour bands for ESI 1–5.
  • Selecting a patient shows the vitals, the shock indicator, the rationale and the AI narrative. Staff can call the next patient and update statuses.
  • A connection pool and autocommit keep database round trips low; a real database failure raises an error, so an outage can never look like an empty queue.

3.13 ARIA Assistant

  • A chat assistant (/api/chat) that answers questions about the patients in the triage queue, reading their records from the database; it can also take an image. It uses the same self-healing model pool and is informational only: it cannot change any triage result.

4. Safety design principles

  • The rules engine is the single source of truth for ESI, shock and priority. AI text is never parsed for numbers (an earlier version did this, and it was removed).
  • Fail safe, not silent: an unreliable respiration reading is shown as "--" (confidence gate at 30%, matched in the display and in the clinical engine) rather than as a plausible-looking wrong number.
  • Any shock flag forces manual review, and so does every patient under 18, because the camera has never been validated on children.
  • Graceful degradation: no ensemble model → hybrid/classical heart rate; no AI model → deterministic narrative; no heart rate → ESI 3 with manual review.
  • Honest validation: every learned model is scored on people it never trained on; changes to the signal code are re-checked against a synthetic self-test (HR error 0.29 BPM) before they are trusted.

5. Validation and results

5.1 Heart rate — 125 recorded sessions, 75 people

Datasets: UBFC-rPPG (50 sessions) and UBFC-Phys (75 sessions: rest, speech and arithmetic tasks). 5.3 Other checks

  • Synthetic self-test: heart rate 0.29 BPM MAE (0.53 RMSE) across 50–140 BPM; synthetic respiration 4.4 br/min (harder than real data, an open item).
  • Clinical engine: unit-tested on 8 hand-built scenarios (normal, isolated tachycardia/tachypnea, borderline and severe shock, bradycardia, low signal, no signal).
  • Frame-rate robustness: HR within about 1–2 BPM of native at 6–120 fps (live resampler test).
  • End-to-end demo: a 52 s test clip (truth 98.3 BPM) gave HR 99.6 BPM, RR 11 at 90% confidence, ESI 3, priority 44/100.
  • Label audit: the reference heart rates for part of the data were found to contain dropouts (0 and 7 BPM readings); corrected labels are used throughout and both versions are reported.

6. Honest limitations

  • No live-webcam study yet. All accuracy numbers come from recorded datasets. Heart rate on a live camera looks plausible, but has not been measured against a reference.
  • Live respiration is not reliable. In one live test the app showed 7 breaths/min at 1% confidence while the subject counted about 22. The baseline-brightness channel locks onto slow drift from the webcam's auto-exposure. It is documented and left unfixed on purpose, because one counted session cannot validate a tuning. Respiration is claimed on recorded data only.
  • Speech and motion remain the hardest conditions: a handful of speech and arithmetic sessions carry about a third of all heart-rate error.
  • Respiration shrinks toward the middle (estimates compress toward 17–18 br/min), which biases toward missing tachypnea, the dangerous direction.
  • Skin-tone accuracy has not been evaluated; the datasets carry no per-subject skin-tone labels. Inclusivity is a design goal, not a result.
  • Children are always sent for manual review; infants' danger limits are above what the signal processing can report.
  • The face tracker is a Haar cascade, which loses the face in some frames; a landmark tracker is available but not deployed.
  • The system is a prototype, not a medical device, and one workstation handles one live patient at a time.

7. Future enhancements

  • Validate on a live webcam against a reference oximeter and counted breathing, then tune on some sessions and test on the rest.
  • Fix live respiration: lock the camera exposure, reject drift-locked readings, and let the live gauge use only recent confident readings.
  • Evaluate across skin tones (Fitzpatrick scale) and across ages, and add children's data before relaxing manual review.
  • Motion-robust signal extraction: a better face tracker (landmarks), and speech-aware handling of the cheeks.
  • More data and models: additional subjects and webcam paced-breathing recordings to train and test the respiration model.
  • Clinical study at one or more sites before any real-world use, including a regulatory review.

8. Technology stack, project map, how to run

Technology stack

  • Frontend: Next.js 16, React, TypeScript, Tailwind CSS
  • Backend: Python, Flask, OpenCV
  • Signal processing: NumPy, SciPy
  • Models: scikit-learn (training), PyTorch on GPU (training only); the app runs numpy only
  • AI layer: CrewAI, NVIDIA-hosted LLMs
  • Data: PostgreSQL on Neon

Where things live

  • core/rppg.py — signal engine
  • core/camera.py — face and ROI
  • core/live_resampler.py — live timing fix
  • core/hr_ensemble.py — HR models
  • core/clinical.py — ESI and shock rules
  • agents/ — crew, tools, model pool
  • web/ — Flask app, routes, database
  • frontend/src/ — dashboard
  • scripts/ — validation and research tools

How to run

  • Backend (port 5002): from the project folder, .\venv\Scripts\python.exe app.py
  • Frontend (port 3000): cd frontend then npm run dev; open http://localhost:3000 in a single tab.
  • A .env file needs NVIDIA_API_KEY and DATABASE_URL. To test without a webcam, upload a recorded video and press Load video.
  • After changing any signal-processing code, run python scripts/validate_dataset.py; it should still report 0.29 BPM synthetic error.

9. Glossary of terms

TermPlain meaning
rPPGRemote photoplethysmography: reading the pulse from tiny colour changes in the skin seen by a camera.
ROIRegion of interest: here, the forehead and cheek patches whose colour is averaged each frame.
CHROM / POSTwo published ways to combine the red, green and blue channels so the pulse stands out and lighting cancels.
Welch spectrumA smoothed frequency chart of the signal; the tallest peak in the heart-rate range is the pulse rate.
HarmonicA copy of the pulse peak at double (or half) its rate; picking it by mistake gives half or double the true heart rate.
SQI / SNRSignal quality index and signal-to-noise ratio: how trustworthy the current reading is.
RIAV / RIIVRespiratory-induced amplitude / intensity variation: how breathing changes the pulse's height and baseline.
HRV (RMSSD)Heart-rate variability: the typical beat-to-beat change in interval length, in milliseconds.
Baevsky stress indexA score of how concentrated the beat intervals are; higher means more autonomic stress.
ESIEmergency Severity Index, the five-level triage scale (1 = most urgent, 5 = least).
Compensated ShockEarly shock where the body keeps blood pressure up by raising heart and breathing rate; flagged here when both are above their limits.
MAEMean absolute error: the average size of the mistake, in the unit measured. Lower is better.
Out-of-foldScoring a model only on people it was not trained on, so the result is not flattering.
EnsembleCombining two or more models; here, the peak selector and the tracker.
